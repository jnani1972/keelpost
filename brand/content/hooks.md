# Opening lines

Patterns for the first line of a post. The writer drafts three openings from these patterns for every piece, uses the best, and keeps the other two in the draft so you can swap.
How to use: add patterns that suit your voice and delete any that do not. The analyst may add rows to the "worked" and "flopped" tables with status `suggested`, citing its metrics report.

## Patterns

| id | pattern | shape | example | best channel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H-01 | plain fact | one short, true, surprising sentence | "Most squeaks come from one bolt." | instagram, facebook |
| H-02 | the reader's problem | name the problem in their words | "Chain slipping on the hill to work?" | instagram, google-profile |
| H-03 | small scene | a moment from the workshop | "A 1987 road bike came in on Tuesday." | linkedin, blog |
| H-04 | myth then truth | a common belief, then what is true | "New bike? Often you just need new brake pads." | linkedin, facebook |
| H-05 | number list | a count and a promise | "Three checks before your winter commute." | blog, email |

## Rules for every opening

1. True and sourced: a fact in an opening needs a source like any other.
2. Stands alone: makes sense if it is the only line someone reads.
3. Under {{fill: e.g. 12}} words.
4. No question the reader would answer "no" to.
5. Follows `brand/identity/tone-rules.md`; no banned words.
